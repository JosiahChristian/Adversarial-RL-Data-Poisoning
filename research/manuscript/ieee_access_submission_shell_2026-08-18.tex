\title{Failure Boundaries of a Trigger-State Q-Margin Detector Under Reward Poisoning in a Toy Tabular-RL Guidance Environment}
\author{Josiah Christian}
\address{Computer Science, Laurel Ridge Community College, 173 Skirmisher Lane, Middletown, VA 22645, USA}
\corresp{Corresponding author: Josiah Christian (e-mail: christianjosiah2484@gmail.com).}
\begin{abstract}Detecting low-strength training-data corruption before obvious policy failure is an important but method-dependent problem in reinforcement learning. We evaluate a trigger-state Q-value-margin detector in an 11-state simulated guidance task trained with tabular Q-learning. The detector threshold was calibrated at poisoning strength 0.15 and frozen for a held-out sweep over 40 policy seeds per strength. At strengths 0.025, 0.050, and 0.100, frozen-threshold recall was 0.225, 0.175, and 0.25, while ROC AUC versus clean policies was 0.425, 0.425, and 0.45; the fraction of trained policies that completed the deterministic task was 1.0, 1.0, and 0.95. Detector discrimination increased at higher strengths as task completion deteriorated. These results support a narrow negative conclusion: this particular trigger-state Q-margin detector provides weak descriptive discrimination in the tested low-strength regime of this toy environment. They do not establish statistical equivalence to chance, general difficulty of detecting subtle poisoning, or impossibility of detection. The trained policy/seed, not the 200 deterministic evaluation episodes used to summarize each policy, is the relevant inferential unit.\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction} Training-time attacks against reinforcement learning span materially different threat models, including adaptive reward poisoning, environment poisoning, online defenses, and backdoor attacks \cite{zhang2020adaptive,rakhsha2020policy,nika2023online,rathbun2025adversarial}. Failure of one feature family under one mechanism cannot establish a general detectability limit. This study evaluates a simple detector with privileged access to the exact trigger states. The trained policy/seed is the experimental unit; reused held-out seeds and a shared clean comparator induce dependence across strengths. External literature establishes threat-model diversity only.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Environment and detector} The experiment uses an 11-state guidance task with tabular Q-learning. Conditions are clean and poisoning strengths 0.025, 0.050, 0.100, 0.150, 0.200, and 0.300. The detector is mean Q-value margin at exact trigger states. Its threshold, 0.8445131510745894, was calibrated at strength 0.15 on seeds 0--19 and frozen.
\subsection{Held-out evaluation} Seeds 40--79 give 40 trained policies per condition. Each policy has 200 deterministic episodes from the same fixed start; these repeat the same trajectory for a fixed greedy Q-table and are not independent learned-policy samples. Mean success rate therefore primarily represents the proportion of trained policies completing the task. The same seeds and clean comparator are reused across strengths, so AUC estimates are correlated.
\subsection{Outcomes and uncertainty} Outcomes are frozen-threshold recall, ROC AUC versus clean, and behavioral summaries including deterministic task completion. The committed JSON contains aggregate summaries. A per-seed CSV was configured for time-limited CI preservation but is not directly recovered in the present package; AUCs are therefore descriptive point estimates and chance-equivalence is not claimed. Provenance-preserving deterministic regeneration from the frozen revision would be reproducibility work if retrieval fails.
\section{Results}
At strength 0.000, 40 clean policies produced zero false positives, specificity 1.0, and completion 1.0. At 0.025, 0.050, and 0.100, recall was 0.225, 0.175, and 0.250; AUC was 0.425, 0.425, and 0.450; completion was 1.000, 1.000, and 0.950. At 0.150, recall/AUC were 0.575/0.675 and completion 0.475. At 0.200 they were 0.800/0.800 and 0.225. At 0.300 recall and AUC were 1.0 while completion was 0.0. Discrimination therefore increased alongside substantial behavioral degradation rather than demonstrating useful early detection.

\section{Negative Result as Contribution} A trigger-state-privileged Q-margin detector fails to provide strong descriptive pre-failure discrimination at strengths 0.025--0.100 in this tested environment. This does not imply that different detectors, representations, attacks, or richer environments must fail; published work uses materially different assumptions \cite{zhang2020adaptive,rakhsha2020policy,nika2023online,rathbun2025adversarial}.
\section{Discussion} Low-strength recall was low and AUC point estimates were below 0.5 while behavior remained near clean. Detector performance improved as policy degradation became pronounced. Forty trained policies, not 8,000 deterministic episodes, define replication per strength. Repeated seeds and the shared clean comparator induce cross-strength dependence. The result is confined to one small simulator, tabular Q-learning, one detector, one calibration procedure, one poisoning design, and the documented seed population.
\section{Limitations} Aggregate summaries do not provide exact seed-level AUC uncertainty. The environment is deliberately small, detector family narrow, calibration occurs at one strength, and the same held-out seeds and comparator are reused across the sweep.
\section{Conclusion} In this 11-state tabular-Q-learning guidance simulator, the tested trigger-state Q-margin detector showed weak descriptive discrimination at strengths 0.025--0.100 while deterministic task completion remained near clean. Separation improved only as degradation became substantial. This is a detector-specific negative result, not evidence that subtle poisoning is generally impossible to detect. Existing per-seed evidence would strengthen uncertainty reporting; broader claims require new prospectively specified studies.
